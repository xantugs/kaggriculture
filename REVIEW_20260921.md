# Review of the latest Kaggriculture agents

Reviewed 21 September 2026. This review uses the latest files in this project, the installed Kaggle Environments 1.32.7 engine, isolated mechanism checks, and two instrumented full matches. It does not establish a new leaderboard score or promote a replacement agent.

## 1. Partial early sales erase the rest of the scheduled order

**Confirmed in A2 and C4.** A2 lines 104–107 shrink an early sale to available stock and then mark the entire future order as done.

An isolated example: the tape schedules `SELL WHEAT 10` next turn. There are three wheat now, so the agent emits a sale of three. Next turn there are seven available, but the original order has disappeared. This is not a pure change of sale timing: it changes requested quantities too.

The behavior occurs in complete games, not just a synthetic example. With seed 210000, A2 in seat 0:

| Opponent | A2 score | Opponent score | Early orders | Partially advanced ordinary orders | Early orders overlapping a same-good pickup request |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Plain champion | 75,737 | 73,834 | 164 | 17 | 21 |
| Reconstructed route 111211276 | 73,375 | 73,012 | 164 | 18 | 22 |

“Ordinary” here means the original request does not exceed the default shed capacity of 100. Each game also had six partial oversized requests. Pickup overlaps are requests, not proof that every pickup or sale failed. Both matches finished with empty inventories; canceled requested quantities must not be reported as permanently lost goods or lost revenue. Later orders can sell that stock.

**Experiment:** replace the boolean completion flag with a remaining-quantity ledger for ordinary orders. Reduce it only by quantities actually executable under the engine's action order. Keep any remainder scheduled for its original turn. Treat oversized “sell everything” orders separately; blindly preserving a nominal remainder of 997 or 1000 is not a useful economic target.

Keep current A2 as the control. Repairing this bookkeeping could improve or reduce scores: the current behavior might accidentally throttle sales at useful times. Measure the effects of changing quantities separately from changing timing.

## 2. The shipped C4 cap never meaningfully constrains an early sale

**Confirmed in the checked-in C4 artifact.** Its `MAXLOT` calculation at lines 24–35 takes the largest requested quantity per good from the tape. Endgame requests make this **1,000 for all nine goods**, while the default shed capacity is 100.

The cap therefore cannot bind a normal early sale from visible inventory. Multipliers of 0.35–0.75 still leave limits of 350–750. This offers a concrete explanation for identical cap sweeps, although the missing historical generator means I cannot establish that every reported sweep used this exact implementation.

Excluding requests equal to 1000 is insufficient: the tape also has quantities such as 992, 997, 998 and 999. They are oversized liquidation requests too.

**Experiment:** use actual filled lots from a trace, or engine market depth, to set a meaningful per-good limit. Sweep a few limits within real inventory scale. First fix or explicitly retain the partial-order semantics; otherwise a tighter cap also deletes more scheduled quantity, confounding the experiment.

The reported conclusion that large windows fail specifically from concentrated selling is not isolated by current code. Larger windows also alter which quantities are canceled and when feed or fertilizer leaves the shed.

## 3. The sales policy uses inventory from before this turn's transfers

**Confirmed ordering mismatch.** The engine applies farmer and hand actions before settling market orders. A2 reads the observation's shed without projecting those actions.

An isolated example emits `PICKUP WHEAT 2` and an early `SELL WHEAT 2` from a shed containing two wheat. A successful pickup consumes the stock first; the sale cannot fill, but A2 has already deleted the later order. The opposite case also matters: a successful deposit can make goods available that the current early-sale policy overlooks.

**Experiment:** implement a small, engine-faithful inventory projection for the current turn. Respect unit order, live positions, carried inventory, and capacity when applying `PICKUP`, `PLACE`, and `DROP`. Keep the route's scheduled orders intact while establishing parity, then calculate surplus after transfers and baseline market actions. Reserve goods needed by upcoming feed/fertilizer pickups.

This changes the sales decision without shifting the production tape. A fixed sequence of unit commands does not guarantee unchanged production if earlier sales deprive later commands of supplies.

## 4. The reported external improvement needs a denominator correction

The table in `RESULTS_sell_timing.md` compares A2's **35/64 = 54.7%** with the plain tape's **24/56 = 42.9%**. A2 gets an extra eight games against its plain parent; the plain row omits its own column.

On the **same seven external opponents**, the table gives:

| Agent | Wins / games | Win rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Plain champion | 24/56 | 42.9% |
| A2 | 27/56 | 48.2% |

That is a reported gain of **5.4 percentage points**, consisting of three additional wins against route 111211276. It is promising, but weaker evidence of general improvement than the headline suggests. The reported 119/120 wins against the parent are a useful separate diagnostic; they do not replace external evaluation.

The separate ergousha result, 10/60 for plain versus 8/60 for A2, does not establish “free, not a trade.” It also does not establish a reliable regression. Raw paired outcomes are needed, and equal aggregate counts elsewhere do not imply identical outcomes on each seed.

Do not discard seat pairing. The local engine consumes shared daily randomness sequentially across farms when spawning weeds; identical market quotes do not guarantee identical trajectories after switching seats. A direct weed-generation probe found a seat difference at seed 402, day 0.

The latest results are prose summaries. The referenced `panelx.py`, `build_tape2.py`, complete rival programs, and corresponding raw match logs were not present in this workspace. The two matches above are mechanism probes on one seed and one seat, not a reproduction of the reported large evaluation.

## Ranked next experiments

Run these as separate candidates so that improvements have an identifiable cause:

1. **Quantity accounting:** A2 plus an ordinary-order remainder ledger. Record scheduled, emitted and filled quantities; keep oversized-order behavior explicit.
2. **Transfer-aware surplus:** add exact current-turn shed projection and short-horizon input reserves. Check whether any feed/fertilizer action loses its supplies and whether total production changes.
3. **Effective sale limits:** test a small number of meaningful per-good caps, with fixed quantity semantics. Measure revenue and remaining stock by good, not just total cash.
4. **Market-aware timing:** test a small sale rule using public inventory, the actual price curve, and the known town-consumption schedule. Begin with one or two goods; compare against A2's constant two-turn horizon. Future opponent orders remain uncertain, so evaluate both quiet and contested market conditions.
5. **Alternative routes for difficult opponents:** if the sales layer plateaus, re-evaluate a small, diverse subset of already saved routes against the same external panel. A route's failure to beat our parent does not prove it is useless against other styles. Avoid treating source rating or parent head-to-head strength as the objective.

I would defer a broad production rewrite, more global route shifts, another cash-triggered V13 handoff, seed buy-ahead sweeps, and generic endgame liquidation. The existing notes already contain negative results for these, and the two new probes ended with no leftover stock. A narrowly defined mechanism would be needed before reopening them.

## A useful promotion test

- Freeze the candidate, current A2, engine version, configurations, and a fixed external opponent panel. Keep parent head-to-head results separate.
- Compare candidate and A2 on identical seed/opponent/seat combinations and equal opponent weights. Report both win points and paired changes in score margin, including each opponent separately.
- Log agent hashes, seeds, seats, scores, terminal statuses, exceptions and timeouts. Missing or failed games must not silently become losses or ties.
- Use whole seed blocks when estimating uncertainty, preserving paired seats and opponent matches together. Do not count all games as independent observations.
- Tune on one seed set, then evaluate one frozen finalist on an untouched set. Previously examined ranking sets are development data even if they are disjoint from an earlier set.
- Choose the sample size from the observed variation and the improvement worth detecting. Neither an arbitrary 100-game threshold nor a smooth parameter curve guarantees a trustworthy conclusion.

No replacement has been validated by this review. Keep A2 as the operational baseline while testing these candidates.
