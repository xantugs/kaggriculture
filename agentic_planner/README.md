# Agentic farm planner: initial implementation

This is a separate research prototype. V12, v11, the existing arena and the Moon experiments are unchanged. Nothing here has been submitted to Kaggle.

## Intended agent

At a planning boundary, reconstruct the observed farm and its commitments; propose several complete work packages; simulate those packages and their continuations against plausible opponent behavior and future worlds; choose a plan; execute its next action; observe what actually happened and revise when necessary.

The language model can assist offline with extracting expert job templates, proposing policy changes and analyzing failed runs. The competition policy should be a fast, self-contained planner. A hosted language-model conversation is not a requirement for autonomous planning.

The existing evidence supports fertilized wheat/carrot rotations, coordinated feed reservations, economical animal retirement and selective replacement of low-value crop work. It does not establish that any simple imitation of elite crop counts will win. Candidate evaluations must include the rival's income and the production displaced by each package.

## Implemented now

- `world.py`: an isolated full-season world using the pinned official interpreter. It includes hires, land, unit actions, ordered market transactions, town consumption, crop growth/decay, animal feeding/care, day resets and storage overflow. It is not restricted to the final seven turns.
- `search.py`: evaluation and ranking of complete reactive candidate policies across the same scenario roots. Each continuation runs to terminal cash. Policies receive only their own private observation. Scores prioritize wins/ties across the modeled scenarios, then average margin. Those scenario results are not calibrated probabilities of real wins.
- `verify.py`: full framework-versus-planning-world integration checks plus a controlled two-crop planning example. The example coordinates input pickup, fertilizer use, watering, harvesting, delivery and selling across multiple days, and includes the rival's sales.
- `verification.json`: generated by the verification command after all checks pass.

The planning example chooses to fertilize both crops when fertilizer is cheap, and to sell fertilizer when its opportunity cost is high. It is a mechanism demonstration, not a full-farm policy or an improvement over v12.

Verification passed on both seeds: 1,150 transitions checked from step 144 through the end, including both private states and final cash. In the controlled demonstration, evaluating one candidate across two 95-turn scenarios took approximately 1.0–2.6 seconds on this host. That exceeds the one-second action budget for just that candidate set; this implementation is currently an offline research tool. Faster state handling, fewer/shorter rollouts with a validated value estimate, or a learned policy distilled from offline search are required before live use.

## Information boundaries

`World.from_observation` accepts only the player's observed state, an explicit estimate of the opponent's private inventory and an independent scenario seed. Configuration seeds are removed at the policy boundary. Scenario randomness is hypothetical; the implementation does not infer the real hidden seed or query future real shops/weeds.

`new_for_validation` and `from_snapshot_for_validation` are separate offline validation interfaces. They can reproduce known test worlds to verify integration with the engine. They must not be used to provide privileged state to a competition policy.

Search forks each world and constructs fresh policies. It does not mutate live observations or a shared v12 instance. An interrupted/failed evaluation is excluded from selection. Its deadline is cooperative, not a hard interrupt of a slow policy callback.

## What is not implemented yet

1. A generator of feasible full-farm job packages from expert trajectories and the current farm.
2. A shared chronological resource ledger and route compiler ensuring all workers can complete those packages.
3. Opponent inventory/behavior models and an appropriately diverse scenario set.
4. A good continuation policy and a cheap, validated terminal-value approximation if full-season search is too slow.
5. Packaging, an action-time budget with a reliable executable fallback, and current-opponent validation.

The old GC code and its latest pinned-world harness are not in this local project snapshot. The older Moon planner is available as a possible source of proposals, but its recorded weaknesses must not be treated as solved by wrapping it in search.

## Next concrete milestone

Extract a complete expert work package including routes, inputs and sales. Compile it into the candidate-policy interface and reproduce its mechanics in this world. Then vary the package, replay the changed consequences through the season and test transfer to held-out towns. Keep v12 as the benchmark. A winning result only in its source world does not pass.

Do not train a large end-to-end policy merely because replays are available. The first useful learning target is ranking feasible packages and proposing promising job schedules; simulation supplies counterfactual outcomes that the recorded games alone do not contain.
