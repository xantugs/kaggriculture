# ML review: repair the action interface, then test continuation RL

Reviewed the supplied Claude transcript and current `moon/bc_core.py`,
`bc_layer.py`, `bc_body.py`, and `feat.py`. This review does not change Claude's
agent/training files or start a remote run. Source hashes are in
`audit_bc_codec.json`.

## Decision

Continue the learning approach, starting with an executable imitation baseline.
The current behavior-cloning network already uses deep learning. Fine-tuning
that neural policy with reinforcement learning would be deep RL. These are
successive training methods, not competing choices.

The reported 0–16 / −$68,213 baseline is a real poor result. It is not yet a clean
measurement of how well this model architecture can imitate the teachers: even
perfect predictions of its training labels cannot reconstruct some teacher
decisions. Fixing this does not establish that the clone will beat v12 or erase
the deficit.

## What I verified

them and supplies perfect encoded teacher labels instead of network outputs.

Deferred sales may recover income, and different decisions may compensate.

Definition: the teacher requests a positive sale; that product has zero initial
shed stock and positive stock after teacher unit actions; the perfect-label
decoder issues no sale for it. This occurs on 201 of 1,456 teacher sale turns
(13.8%) in this small sample. It is evidence of recurring representation failure,
not a measured win-rate or cash improvement from repairing it.

The quantity bins also change the requested amount of 394/1,084 teacher pickup
commands. This is a requested-quantity count: available stock can clamp both
requests to the same executed amount, so it must not be reported as 394 failed
pickups.

Relevant implementation locations:

- `bc_core.py:63`: selective PLACE loses item/quantity information.
- `bc_core.py:82`: independent market totals discard sequence and repeated slots.
- `bc_layer.py:127`: sales use initial shed stock rather than stock after unit actions.
- `bc_layer.py:124`: purchase budgeting ignores earlier sale proceeds.
- `bc_layer.py:162`: purchases always precede sales, then the list is truncated.
- `bc_body.py:187`: reported unit accuracy checks action class, not quantity.
- `bc_body.py:102`: the split shuffles teacher seats, so both seats from one
  recording can appear in different splits. Retain episode IDs and group them.

The market head also does not receive the individual workers' carried inventory
vectors or selected unit actions. It should see delivery information when
predicting sales. There are further joint-legality limitations, such as testing
each worker against the original tile state. Their cost has not been measured.

## Minimum next build

1. **Make teacher commands reproducible.** Encode item and quantity for deposits
   and pickups; preserve ordered market slots, their quantities and a stop token.
   Generate decisions sequentially with shared-resource accounting. Project unit
   transfers before sale sizing. Purchases funded by previous sales must remain
   expressible; account for actual game order and uncertain opponent prices.
   Do not replace exact quantities with another untested set of coarse bins.

2. **Require a perfect-label reconstruction gate.** On held-out recordings,
   encode/decode teacher actions and compare the resulting official-engine
   transition with the original action at the same state and opponent action.
   Compare money, inventory, workers, tiles and market, not raw command equality
   alone: some different commands are equivalent. Start with the five regressions
   above, then expand to representative complete recordings. Resolve consequential
   mismatches before scaling training.

3. **Rebuild the imitation baseline.** Group train/validation/test by episode,
   keeping both seats together. Report full command/quantity accuracy and
   precision/recall on nonzero market decisions alongside per-class accuracy.
   Verify training versus exported inference on identical observations. Include
   workers' inventory and sufficient crop/animal timing information in the input.
   Test short continuations and record the first economically material departure.
   Recurrent memory and teacher-style conditioning are possible follow-up
   experiments, not substitutes for interface correctness.

4. **Run BC-initialized PPO on continuations.** Start from saved late-season
   states, initially around days 27–28, then expand toward days 24, 21 and 15 as
   execution and results improve. This is a curriculum for the same policy
   controlling all workers and the market, not the failed extra-worker patch.
   Include v12-generated takeover states as well as elite states; otherwise the
   learner may only work on farms it cannot reach. Keep a modest imitation loss
   or reference-policy constraint while learning recovery through actual play.
   Preserve v12's opening for the initial deployable day-15 variant.

5. **Train against reacting opponents and measure the right outcome.** Use v12,
   v11 and frozen earlier learner checkpoints in both seats. Static elite action
   tapes are useful for reconstruction, not a sufficient closed-loop opponent
   benchmark. Hold out worlds and periodically evaluate full games, including
   the opening/takeover boundary. Main evidence: paired win rate versus v12,
   margin, catastrophic-loss frequency and uncertainty across seeds. A PPO model
   merely beating the present weak clone is not submission evidence.

No in-game policy may observe the real future shops/weeds, hidden opponent
inventory or simulator seed. Offline snapshots may restore full engine state,
but the policy input must retain the competition observation boundary. Evaluate
new future scenarios to catch memorization of training continuations.

## Compute and reward corrections

The reported evaluation throughput is 16 games / 634 seconds, approximately 91
games per wall-clock hour for that whole evaluation setup. It does not establish
the engine-only speed, GPU-batched training throughput, or a mandatory number of
games needed to learn. Profile engine, observation copying, opponent and policy
inference separately before estimating a training budget. Batch inference over
several active environments; do not begin by porting the whole engine or assume
batching will remove an unmeasured bottleneck. Report actual transitions/second
and validation improvement per elapsed hour from the first pilot.

Daily cash differences do not inherently reward hoarding. With no discounting
or clipping, their sum is final cash minus starting cash. But maximizing our own
cash ignores the rival's gain, and expected cash margin differs from win rate.
Use terminal win/loss as the primary objective. If adding dense guidance, use
potential differences `gamma * Phi(next_state) - Phi(state)` with zero terminal
potential and consistent terminal/truncation handling. This preserves the base
objective in the standard fixed-opponent setting; it does not guarantee easy
optimization or good play. Arbitrary positive harvest/feed bonuses are a
different objective and can encourage unprofitable activity.

Static replay data also does not give access to the elite teacher on newly
visited states. Do not describe an offline replay pipeline as full DAgger.
Interactive teacher labels require a callable expert, and v12's fixed routes
should not be assumed competent on arbitrary off-route farms.
