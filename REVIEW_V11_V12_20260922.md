## Finding 1: two sale reorderers assume a mirror, without requiring one

At lines 6031 and 6172, `_V44Y_REORDER_GATE = False` means the older reorderer runs without its available clone check. `_v44y_reorder` copies our queue as the opponent queue (line 6140); its evaluator gives the opponent our stock (lines 6112–6114).

The newer `_slot_act` also assumes the opponent uses the incoming parent order, with equal sale lots (lines 7277 onward). Its wrapper does not require a clone classification. The final-day controller likewise consults our own parent queue as a forecast of the rival.

An isolated executable counterexample used market inventories of 10,000 and our shed containing 10 strawberries, 40 milk and 20 wool. SLOT changed our order from strawberry/milk/wool to milk/wool/strawberry. Against an opponent selling only 30 strawberries, exact per-unit lockstep evaluation reduced our cash margin by $958. This is a synthetic demonstration of model mismatch, not a measured loss from the ladder or evidence that disabling SLOT generally helps.

**Experiment:** ablate the two reorderers separately and together, conditionally on sustained divergence of the rival's production and observed sale behavior. Retain their existing behavior against credible mirrors. A later improvement can evaluate candidate queues against several plausible rival queues, estimated stock ranges and the exact marginal price curve. Do not use the actual hidden simultaneous action as a live input.

**Budget:** half a day to one day for instrumentation and gated ablations. Stop if held-out strong-opponent improvements are outweighed by lost clone-family wins. The existing clone similarity gate is a starting feature, not a complete classifier: matching tiles need not imply matching cargo or sale policy.

## Finding 2: WFEED is useful but is not a chronological feed guarantee

At lines 7495–7515, WFEED totals future FEED requests and anticipated harvest income until a pickup, drop or day boundary, then compares the totals. It does not ensure wheat exists before each individual FEED. Future yields are read from the current board, without advancing other workers' harvests. Additions are capped at three and require spare wheat already in the shed.

The diagnostic constructs a legal local schedule: PICKUP one wheat; FEED the first animal; move and FEED a second animal; then harvest four wheat. Ten wheat were available in the shed. WFEED still requests only one because it counts the later harvest against both earlier feeds. Running the returned schedule through the official unit engine confirms the second animal remains unfed, with four wheat collected afterwards.

This edge case was not observed in v12's four smoke games. It establishes a limitation, not its prevalence or value.

WFEED is also the outermost wrapper: its pickup changes happen after the earlier market projections and supply guards. Those earlier checks therefore do not certify the final returned action. FGUARD forecasts the next raw tape's fertilizer pickups, rather than a fully simulated future wrapped policy.

**Experiment:** first log actual unsuccessful FEED/FERTILIZE actions in recent strong games. Replace total-based pickup sizing with chronological inventory simulation, and reconcile the final action's input reservations before allowing sales. Simulate all involved workers together so a wheat harvest is not promised twice. Preserve future pickup funding, warehouse capacity and action order.

**Budget:** about one day for a narrow repair, longer for a multi-day reservation system. Keep only changes that recover actual failures or unlock profitable, verified crop changes. A synthetic fix alone does not warrant promotion.

## Finding 3: herd adaptation has a concrete asymmetry worth testing

There is already more town adaptation than the phrase 'fixed tape' suggests. `_hd2_ev` forecasts animal products and includes an approximate effect on rival revenue. However, `_hd2_decide` primarily considers replacing scheduled GOOSE purchases with COW/SHEEP. The later `_y_target` has `yarngeese=True`: a yarn shop can turn a scheduled goose into a sheep without comparing egg demand or the projected wool surplus in that decision (lines 6181, 6193–6200).

**First experiment:** preserve scheduled geese in mixed yarn/egg towns when a marginal comparison favors eggs. Compare the actual scheduled group's future egg and wool sales, revealing only already-open shops and using observed rival animals. Include feed, purchase prices, existing output, price impact, collection timing and possible storage loss. There is no need to add land or workers just to test retaining the original goose plan.

**Second experiment, only if the first supports it:** replace one scheduled cow/sheep purchase with a goose where the existing visits support it. This requires coordinated BUILD_COOP/PICKUP/PLACE and sale changes, not just renaming a purchase. Official animal storage limits are four eggs versus six milk/wool: a sheep collection schedule can waste goose output. Reject any branch whose collection cadence cannot capture the eggs economically.

**Budget:** one to two days for the conservative branch. The reported egg deficit motivates this experiment; it does not establish that the substitutions will close it.

## Finding 4: carrot adaptation uses current prices and aggregate feed stock

The crop layer is active from day 6 through day 28, not solely days 10–23 as summarized in the handoff. `_ca_yield_path` already checks approximate future visits and yields. But the economic comparison at lines 4649 and 4669 prices the harvest at current carrot and wheat quotes. The feed gate compares total carried-plus-shed wheat with one day's tape FEED requests (line 4661).

A credible improvement is to price each feasible rotation at its expected delivery time, integrating the marginal sale price for the whole lot and estimating known shop consumption and rival supply. Crucially, safe feed delivery must be established for the workers that need it, not just the farm-wide quantity. This is materially different from simply lowering the carrot threshold or reserve, both already reported to have failed.

**Budget:** one to two days after resource accounting is reliable. Start with existing wheat/carrot rotations and unchanged routes. Tomatoes should come later, with an explicit watering/fertilizer/harvest/transport schedule and displaced work valued.

## Correction to the earlier continuation-search recommendation

The embedded `_PLANNER_NS['simulate']` at source line 1022 is explicitly restricted to steps 712–718, rejects day boundaries, accepts SELL-only market orders and does not simulate shared prices. Calling it at step 240 produced `Unsupported: outside 712..718; no day boundary or terminal auto-drop`.

It is not an existing full-season simulator ready for day-15 policy search. The standalone official engine provides a foundation, but continuing the complete wrapped agent requires isolating all its mutable state, simulating day transitions and resource flows, and representing unknown opponent stock/actions and future shops. Copying only the underlying chassis does not clone the policy's many wrapper states. Therefore the earlier 2–3-day estimate for a robust continuation-search system was too optimistic.

Use a restricted offline search first: one feasible rotation or one scheduled animal group, with v12 as the baseline. Evaluate complete consequences through the season rather than scoring only immediate crop revenue. Do not spend the entire remaining week building a general scheduler before finding a transferable improvement.

## Priority and evidence needed

1. Preserve v12 as the baseline; its guards demonstrably help in the smoke sample.
2. Run mirror-conditioned market ablations as the cheapest potential source of additional close wins.
3. Measure and repair chronological resource failures; use the corrected accounting to support production changes.
4. Test conditional goose preservation and forecast-priced wheat/carrot rotations as the smallest production branches.
5. Escalate to a new second half only after an offline package demonstrates gains across different towns.

Use development and held-out towns, both seats, several opponent families, and the official unchanged engine for final acceptance. Prefix replays can diagnose specific losses but cannot establish performance against an adapting private opponent. Include previous wins in every comparison; optimizing only selected losses can produce a worse agent.

Neither this review nor the four smoke games establishes a gold probability, a +75 rating gain, or a $10k improvement. The refined conclusion is that there are identifiable, testable opportunities between another parameter tweak and a complete rewrite.
